Growing up in Calgary, Alberta, I spent time mountain biking, hiking, and skiing in the Canadian Rocky Mountains. As my outdoor experience grew, I ventured further into the backcountry and started bringing a camera to share my experiences. This has become my preferred style of photography: finding awe-inspiring landscapes, often far in the mountains.

Professionally, I have had a few different careers. In 2011, I graduated from civil engineering at the University of Alberta and worked as a structural engineer-in-training for just over three years. In 2019, I earned an MSc. in biomedical engineering from the University of Calgary. My thesis, “[Intercellular Gap Junction Communication in the Bovine Annulus Fibrosus](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/handle/1880/109915),” investigated cell-cell signalling in the intervertebral disc.

As far back as high school, I enjoyed tinkering with web development. I remember drafting mockups in Photoshop, splicing them into HTML table-based layouts, then creating custom WordPress themes. Does anyone else recall coding a website that rendered correctly in Firefox, Chrome, and Safari, only to have it explode in Internet Explorer 6?

After many years of career uncertainty, I decided to embrace my inner code monkey, making a career pivot by taking the Lighthouse Labs web development boot camp in 2019 (see [My Journey into Tech](https://russellmcwhae.ca/journal/my-journey-into-tech)). This brought my fiddling with websites from my high-school days full circle: web development has become my full-time career for over five years now.

A lot has changed in twenty years of building websites. I never imagined “vibe coding” would become possible in my lifetime, yet we now have coding agents that can parse thousand-file legacy codebases and add complicated new features, or build entire apps, only from a brief text description. Work that would have taken months previously now takes mere minutes—seconds, in some cases.

Some things haven’t changed, however. Content will always be king, and good design stands out. “AI slop” is but the next iteration of clickbait. In my early days of studying graphics design, I remember learning to keep iterating until a design feels right. This has been only amplified by agentic coding: if something doesn’t look right, keep going until you get to the right level of polish.

My career change took me to Revelstoke in 2020, where I lived for five years. (See my journal for some more detailed reflections: [A New Chapter](https://russellmcwhae.ca/journal/a-new-chapter), and [11 Months Later](https://russellmcwhae.ca/journal/11-months-later).) In 2025, I moved farther west, to the Vancouver coast.

As my creative outlet, this website is about seeing: seeing the images of the wild places I have visited, and seeing the world through my eyes via the prose I have written. I hope you enjoy your time here—a quiet corner of the noisy world.
