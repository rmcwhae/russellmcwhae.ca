Growing up in Calgary, Alberta, I spent time mountain biking, hiking and skiing in the Canadian Rocky Mountains. As my outdoor experience grew, I began to venture further into the backcountry and started bringing a camera to share my experiences in hard-to-reach places. This has become my preferred style of photography: finding awe-inspiring landscapes, often far in the mountains.

Professionally, I have had a few different careers. In 2011, I graduated from civil engineering at the University of Alberta and worked as a structural engineer-in-training for just over three years. In early 2019, I earned an MSc. in biomedical engineering from the University of Calgary. My thesis, “[Intercellular Gap Junction Communication in the Bovine Annulus Fibrosus](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/handle/1880/109915),” investigated cell-cell signalling in the intervertebral disc.

In a [career pivot into tech](/journal/my-journey-into-tech), I took the Lighthouse Labs web development boot camp in 2019. I have been working full time as a web developer for over five years now, though it has been over twenty years that I have been building websites.

I cut my teeth back in the days of Internet Explorer 6 and have kept up with the evolution of the web over the years. A lot has changed—I never imagined vibe coding would become possible in my lifetime—but a few things haven’t. Content will always be king, and good design stands out. In my early days of studying graphics design, I remember learning to keep iterating until a design feels right. This has been only amplified by agentic coding: if something doesn’t look right, keep steering until you get to something polished.

My journey took me to Revelstoke in 2020, where I lived for five years. In 2025, I moved farther west, to the Vancouver coast.

Similar to photography, web development requires a keen technical understanding. Coding has always come naturally to me, but it is the combination of the logic of coding and the creative side of graphics design that makes the web a rewarding medium to create with.

Photography and design are complementary skills. Design principles provide an expanded tool kit to use in photography. Similarly, being able to take photos allows me to readily incorporate them into my design work.
