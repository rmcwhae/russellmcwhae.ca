I grew up in Calgary, Alberta, exploring the Canadian Rocky Mountains. Those early adventures sparked a lifelong passion for the outdoors—on foot, two wheels, skis, and, eventually, surfboards.

At the same time, I was dabbling in web development. In the early 2000s, that meant drafting mockups in Photoshop, slicing them into HTML tables, and building custom WordPress themes. (Shoutout to any developers who remember battling Internet Explorer 6 to get a webpage to render correctly.)

As my outdoor experience grew, I ventured deeper into the backcountry and started bringing a camera to capture what I saw. This evolved into my preferred style of photography: expansive natural landscapes, often found deep in the mountains.

My professional path, like so many of my outdoor adventures, took a few unexpected turns. In 2011, I graduated with a degree in civil engineering from the University of Alberta and worked as a structural engineer-in-training for just over three years. Following a layoff, I returned to school and earned an MSc in biomedical engineering from the University of Calgary in 2019. My thesis, “[Intercellular Gap Junction Communication in the Bovine Annulus Fibrosus](https://prism.ucalgary.ca/handle/1880/109915),” investigated cell-cell signalling in the intervertebral disc.

After years of career uncertainty, I decided to embrace my inner coder and enrolled in the Lighthouse Labs web development boot camp in 2019 (see [My Journey into Tech](https://russellmcwhae.ca/journal/my-journey-into-tech)). That high-school hobby has since become my full-time career—and one I truly enjoy. Technology combines many of the things that have always appealed to me: logical problem-solving, visual design, and the satisfaction of turning ideas into reality.

That career pivot, combined with my love of the outdoors, brought me to Revelstoke in 2020, where I spent five years. (See my journal for some more detailed reflections: [A New Chapter](https://russellmcwhae.ca/journal/a-new-chapter), and [11 Months Later](https://russellmcwhae.ca/journal/11-months-later).) In 2025, I moved farther west to the coast of British Columbia.

This website is my creative outlet—a place to share the world as I see it through photographs and words. Thanks for stopping by my quiet corner of a noisy internet.
